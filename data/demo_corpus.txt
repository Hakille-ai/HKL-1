HKL-1 is a bare-metal neuromorphic AI engine written in Rust.
HKL-2 extends HKL-1 with eligibility propagation and spiking transformer self-attention.
It runs on microcontrollers with zero external dependencies and zero heap allocations.
Neuromorphic computing processes information using spatio-temporal spikes.
The brain uses dopamine and serotonin to regulate learning and synaptic plasticity.
Spiking neural networks achieve massive energy efficiency compared to standard DNNs.
Eligibility propagation enables online learning without backpropagation through time.
The spiking transformer uses binary attention over spike trains.
Multi-modal sensory fusion combines text, audio, and vision into a unified 512D representation.
The metacognitive auto-tuner adjusts learning rate and surrogate gradients dynamically.
Federated learning allows swarm nodes to share knowledge without central coordination.
eFPGA compilation freezes stable subnetworks into synthesizable Verilog RTL.
The native HKL protocol enables real-time inference and training over TCP.
